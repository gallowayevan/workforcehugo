The graphical-integrity rule

Added as a core principle after review, and it applies to the design system itself, not just article content:

Every chart-shaped mark on the site renders real, labeled data — or it doesn't exist.

No decorative sparklines, no illustrative bars, no placeholder maps. Card imagery is either a real figure from the post (as today), or there is no image and typography carries the card. If the homepage ever shows a county grid or trend line, it is generated from current HPDS data at build time and carries a source line, exactly like a figure in a report. Until that pipeline exists, the hero uses verified numbers only.

The freshness rule: a stale number is a fake chart with a delay.

Dated content may carry frozen numbers — a post's key statistic is its finding, fixed at publication like a headline; the visible date is the context. Undated surfaces (homepage, section pages) show only numbers wired to a single data file updated during the annual HPDS refresh — never hand-typed into a template. If a number can't be wired that way, it doesn't appear on an evergreen page.
